<Section id="sec-1" title="Introduction">
<p>The sprint review is the ceremony where we close out a sprint and assess what happened. Its value depends entirely on the quality of the information it surfaces. A review that simply lists completed and incomplete tasks tells us what happened but not why, and offers little guidance for the next sprint.</p>

<p>The ambition behind this document is to build the best sprint review we can by grounding it in a clear measurement model. The approach is to identify a small set of base measures—raw values recorded directly from the sprint—and then derive from them a set of metrics that answer the questions that matter most: did we deliver what we committed to, did the sprint scope remain stable, and was our capacity estimate realistic.</p>

<p>By separating base measures from derived metrics, the model stays transparent. The raw data is always visible, and the calculated indicators are traceable back to the values they depend on. This makes it possible to understand not just the numbers, but what they mean and where they come from.</p>
</Section>

<Section id="sec-2" title="Concepts">
<p>The sprint review model rests on a small vocabulary of scope categories. Each category describes a subset of sprint tasks based on when and how they entered or left the sprint.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Committed Scope</strong> — The tasks that were in the sprint at the moment the sprint plan was committed. This is the original baseline: the work we said we would do.</li>
  <li><strong>Added Scope</strong> — Tasks added to the sprint after commitment. These represent mid-sprint scope expansion, whether due to urgent incoming work, adaptation to new information, or replacement of removed tasks.</li>
  <li><strong>Removed Scope</strong> — Tasks that were originally committed but later removed from the sprint. A task may be removed because it was deprioritized, blocked, no longer relevant, or replaced by higher-priority work.</li>
  <li><strong>Final Scope</strong> — The tasks that remain in the sprint at the end, regardless of whether they were completed. Final Scope equals Committed Scope minus Removed Scope plus Added Scope.</li>
</ul>
</Section>

<Section id="sec-3" title="Base Measures">
<p>Base measures are the raw values recorded from a sprint. Each measure is captured in two units: points (estimated effort) and tasks (count). Points reflect how much work was expected; tasks reflect how many discrete objectives were involved. Together, they provide a complete picture of the sprint's scope and outcomes.</p>

<p>For each base measure, the points and tasks are broken down into four sub-values: how many were completed, how many were not completed, how many were removed from the sprint, and the total. The removed column does not apply to Final Scope because it represents scope at the end, after removals have already been accounted for.</p>

<Section id="sec-4" title="Derived Metrics">
<p>Derived metrics are calculated from the base measures. Each one answers a specific question about the sprint. They are expressed as percentages for both points and tasks, and the two versions often tell different stories: a sprint can complete most of the estimated effort while leaving many small tasks unfinished, or complete many tasks while missing the few that carried the most weight. Comparing point-based and task-based values reveals whether the sprint was dominated by a few heavy items or many light ones.</p>
